Prokofiev's violin works span his whole career and show three different composers. The Violin Concerto No. 1 (Op. 19, 1917) is the strange, dreamy one — written during the revolutionary years, premiered only in 1923, opening with a quietly floating melody that doesn't quite land anywhere. The Violin Concerto No. 2 (Op. 63, 1935) is the Soviet-era one: warmer, more direct, the kind of piece that sounds approachable until you look at what the violin is actually doing. Then there is the Violin Sonata No. 1 (Op. 80, 1946) — dark, long, and monumental, one of the great chamber works of the 20th century, which Prokofiev is not often given credit for writing.

Itzhak Perlman is probably the finest modern interpreter of this repertoire — big tone, total authority, and enough warmth to make the lyrical writing land without sentimentality. His recording of the Concerto No. 2 with the BBC Symphony is the one to start with.
