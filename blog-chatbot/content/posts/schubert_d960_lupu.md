Radu Lupu (1945–2022) was famously reclusive — few recordings, almost no interviews, a public career conducted as if publicity were a mild embarrassment. He won the Leeds, the Van Cliburn, and the George Enescu competitions in the late 1960s and then largely refused to behave like someone who had. At the keyboard he never pushed a phrase toward you.

His recording of Schubert's final piano sonata, D.960 — the opening movement's long, suspended melody arrives without urgency, as if he simply found it there and decided not to disturb it.
