Just being curious, so a quick Google tells me: exchange rate is the price of currencies so technically it should also follow the supply and demand rule. Different mechanism operates in different time magnitude:

- The purchasing power parity theory: a burger costs 6 in Chicago and 600 in Tokyo then the EX rate should be 0.01
- The trade clock: a country running a big trade deficit is defacto selling its own currency, which should push its rate down til the trade dicifit self-correct.
- The asset clock: global trade in goods and services runs maybe $30-odd trillion *a year*. FX turnover is $12 trillion *a day*. Trade-related currency demand is a rounding error; the overwhelming majority of currency trading is people buying currencies in order to buy *assets*.
