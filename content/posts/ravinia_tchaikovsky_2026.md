Ravinia has closed its summer season with an all-Tchaikovsky evening for nearly fifty years. The CSO spoiled us on its 90th summer residency here — four Tchaikovsky pieces in one night that would take four separate concerts to hear during the regular season.

**Earl Lee**, conductor | **Stella Chen**, violin

Tchaikovsky — Polonaise from *Eugene Onegin*, op. 24<br>
Tchaikovsky — Violin Concerto in D major, op. 35<br>
Tchaikovsky — *Romeo & Juliet* Fantasy Overture<br>
Tchaikovsky — *1812* Overture, op. 49 (with cannons)

---

The Polonaise from *Eugene Onegin* opened the evening without the weight of the opera behind it — just the grand, cheerful march on a sunny Sunday afternoon, which is exactly what it should be. The CSO's brass was the story of the night: full-bodied, bright, locked together in tone, never missing a note. Tchaikovsky writes for brass as if he trusts them completely, and Sunday they earned it.

Stella Chen's tone in the Violin Concerto was genuinely lovely. The first movement was a bit rushed — one note in the wrong key, some nerves, the outdoor acoustics doing her no favors — but she settled into the second and third movements and they were great. The Canzonetta especially. The Ravinia pavilion is not an easy room for a soloist to calibrate.

---

The second half was the spectacle. *Romeo & Juliet* built steadily, the love theme properly expansive in the open air. Before the finale, Earl Lee shared that Ravinia was the first job he applied to after college — as a cellist. He didn't get it. He stood there Sunday night as the conductor closing the CSO's 90th summer residency. Not a bad arc.

Then the *1812* Overture — the cannons were brought out, and they did their job.
