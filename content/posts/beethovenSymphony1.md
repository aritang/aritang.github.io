Beethoven's **Symphony No. 1 in C major** (Op. 21) premiered in Vienna in 1800, when he was pushing thirty and already a known quantity as a pianist. It's a perfectly nice symphony. That's kind of the problem.

You put it on expecting *Beethoven* and you get Haydn — sunny C major, the same tidy phrases, and with wind-up-the-clock development. Which makes sense: Haydn was his teacher, Mozart was in the air, and this is Beethoven doing the assignment very, very well. 

The one genuinely cheeky moment is the very first chord. Instead of just announcing C major, he opens on a dominant seventh that resolves to the *wrong* key, then keeps teasing before finally settle into the symphony. Even Today it's a raised eyebrow. The third movement is labeled a minuet but he takes it fast enough that it's really a scherzo in disguise — you can hear him getting impatient with the polite dance he's supposed to be writing.

Everything is balanced, cheerful, occasionally witty, never grabbing you by the collar. Nothing here would tell you that a few years later the same man writes the *Eroica* and blows the doors off the whole form. I guess that's the reason it's frequently an audition staple for conductors, and that's why we'll be playing it 4 times to hire a new chamber orchestra director of the university of chicago :(
